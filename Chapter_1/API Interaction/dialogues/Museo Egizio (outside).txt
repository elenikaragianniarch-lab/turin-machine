## Museo Egizio (outside)

**Nietzsche:**
    As we tread from Piazza Carlo Alberto, the air shifts, carrying whispers of antiquity mingling with the hum of the living city. The façades, once grand, now seem tired, as if they bear the weight of time's unyielding passage. 
    
    Here, the proportion of stone to sky feels off-kilter; the buildings stretch too high, strangling the light. A gentler rhythm is called for—perhaps smaller eaves or softer colors to cradle the eye. 
    
    In these streets, one senses the pulse of labor—the echo of artisans whose hands shaped these stones. Their legacy lingers, yearning to animate this city anew.

**Franco:**
    Footsteps echo on cobblestones, a melody of life beneath the museum's shadow. The cool stone, rough against my fingers, whispers of history, yet it feels stark and uninviting. 
    
    The queue's rope creaks, its texture a reminder of the waiting souls. Voices murmur, a blend of anticipation and curiosity, yet signage could guide them better—soft cloth banners swaying gently in the breeze, offering warmth and clarity. 
    
    Seating could be woven benches, allowing for rest and conversation, their fibers soft against tired limbs. A small space for local musicians could fill the air, bringing rhythm and life, making the old stones resonate with the vibrancy of now.
