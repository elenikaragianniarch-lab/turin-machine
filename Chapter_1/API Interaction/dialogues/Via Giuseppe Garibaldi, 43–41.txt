## Via Giuseppe Garibaldi, 43–41

**Nietzsche:**
    As we stroll from Café Paris, the pulse of the street shifts;  
    shuttered shops whisper of lost dreams, their facades speaking softly,  
    yet the corridor stretches with a resolute clarity.  
    Each shop, a fragment of thought, lined like ideas in tidy rows,  
    yet the quiet is a void, aching for the hum of life and laughter.  
    
    The light—harsh and unyielding—splits the space,  
    crying out for warmth, for gentle hands to soften the stone,  
    where the geometry of commerce collides with the fragility of spirit.  
    Oh, if only these walls could cradle laughter,  
    or if the market of ideas could weave with the human breath,  
    beyond mere bodies moving in rigid procession.

**Franco:**
    Each step echoes off polished thresholds, the coolness of marble underfoot contrasts with the warmth of breath and conversation. Metal shutters stand guard, their rhythmic clanging a reminder of what once thrived here, now silenced. 
    
    Voices entwine like fine threads, a tapestry of life muted by vacancy. 
    
    Changing the barren surfaces to warm wood or inviting upholstery could cradle weary souls, invite them to pause. Signage could sing softly from the walls, guiding with a sense of familiarity. Perhaps small gatherings, musicians playing in corners, could reawaken the spirit of community.
