## Piazza Carlo Alberto

**Nietzsche:**
    As we glide from the Palazzo Carignano, the weight of baroque elegance shifts,  
    The air thickens with the pulse of industry,  
    Brick and stone whisper of labor, of hands shaping dreams and despair.  
    The streets narrow, yet the skies above widen,  
    A contrast, like compassion coaxing reason to soften its gaze.  
    
    I long for a gentler touch upon these façades—  
    Perhaps a warm hue, a blend of earth and sun,  
    To calm the cold stone, to cradle the heart in its embrace.  
    A ritual of pause, a moment to breathe,  
    While machines hum in the distance, we must remember the art of stillness.

**Franco:**
    The benches here feel rough against my fingers, splintered wood betraying time and neglect. A soft drip echoes from the statue above; water finds its way down, a reminder of impermanence. Students shuffle, their notebooks rustling like leaves in autumn—thoughts spilling out in ink.
    
    A warm, sun-kissed color on the benches would invite more to rest, to linger in conversation. Perhaps a gentle curve in the stone for better comfort, embracing the weary. Small gatherings could bloom, with flowers on tables, spreading joy and connection among the industrious hum around us.
