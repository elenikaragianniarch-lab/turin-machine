## Monumental Arch to the Artillery Force

**Nietzsche:**
    As we descend from the Gran Madre, the air thickens with the weight of history, yet softens with the whispers of the river below. The steps, once resolute, now feel uneven, as if the city itself is weary from its own grandeur. 
    
    The ochre façades stand in mute testimony to grandeur, but they could cradle life more tenderly. Where are the gardens among the stone? A riot of green could weave through this granite, softening the sharpness of memory with the gentleness of each season. 
    
    I sense the pulse of labor beneath the surface; the artisans who carved the very stones of this city, their hands worn yet skilled. A ritual of creation, buried beneath the weight of military memory—where is the reverence for the maker, the care for the craft? 
    
    What if these monuments echoed laughter, the echoes of life dancing among the shadows? Shall we not invite the everyday, to soften the obedient silence of the past?

**Franco:**
    The air here hums with the warm thrum of engines, a deep bass that vibrates through the stone—a heartbeat of the city. Grit clings to my palm as I rest against the cool surface of the Monumental Arch. Each wreath’s detail prickles under my fingers—metal entwined with leaves, a touch of nature clasping history.
    
    The traffic flows like a river, yet it feels impersonal, a barrier to connection. If only a few benches, made of smooth wood, could dot the edges, inviting weary souls to pause and breathe. Perhaps strings of delicate lights could drape among the branches of newly planted trees, casting a soft glow as dusk settles.
    
    Why not add signs crafted from warm bronze, guiding visitors with tactile maps? Let the arch echo with laughter, local musicians playing nearby, weaving joy into the fabric of history. A celebration of craft and life, diverse and rich, would breathe warmth into the stone's cold embrace.
