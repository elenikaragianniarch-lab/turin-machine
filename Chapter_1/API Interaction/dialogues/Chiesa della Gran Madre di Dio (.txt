## Chiesa della Gran Madre di Dio (steps)

**Nietzsche:**
    As we traverse from the square, the air shifts with a whisper of the river—a palpable promise of life. The façades, heavy with history, loom like sentinels, yet they seem unforgiving in their grandeur.
    
    The cobblestones beneath our feet, uneven and worn by the passage of countless souls, speak of labor—a testament to both toil and beauty entwined. Here, the ascent to Chiesa della Gran Madre is not merely physical; it feels like an ascent of the spirit, a journey begging for a gentler touch.
    
    What if we softened the harsh lines of industry with verdant growth, allowed ivy to reclaim the stone? Rituals of forgiveness could be inscribed upon the walls, reminders of kindness in the very fabric of this city.

**Franco:**
    Gulls caw overhead, their cries echoing off the stone, sharp and lively. The scent of damp earth and incense from nearby offerings fills the air, mingling with the cool mist that clings to everything. Each step up feels sturdy yet unforgiving, the stone edges like an invitation to stumble.
    
    A bench made of warm wood could offer rest, smoothing the hard edges of the steps. Perhaps a gentle curve to the railings, inviting touch rather than mere grasp. Signage could speak in soft tones—braille alongside clear lettering, guiding the way, making the space more welcoming for all. 
    
    What if we had small gatherings here, musicians playing heartening tunes, weaving sound into the fabric of the place?
