## Piazza delle Erbe

**Nietzsche:**
    As we stroll from Garibaldi's pulse, the air shifts softly,  
    The grand façades yield to modest corners,  
    A whisper of labor clings to the cobblestones,  
    Each step inches closer to the heart—  
    The exchange in the market still echoes,  
    A rhythm of voices, a dance of barter,  
    Life here teeters on the edge of beauty and decay.  
    
    The shadows of yesterday linger,  
    While the promise of what can be  
    Insists upon the perishable nature of all things.  
    Might we, gentle souls, alter the materials,  
    Softening the harshness of stone and iron,  
    With a caress of warmth and compassion?

**Franco:**
    Cold stone greets my fingertips, sharp edges rough against my palms. The air carries scents of fresh basil and citrus, hints of sweetness mingling with something earthy. A canvas flap flutters nearby, catching the wind like an old friend returning. 
    
    Consider wooden benches, broad and inviting, their surfaces smooth to the touch, allowing weary travelers respite. A patch of soft grass or fragrant herbs could soften the starkness of cobbles, a gentle reminder of nature's embrace. 
    
    A sign with raised letters, perhaps near the fountain, could guide the lost. Tiny events, like fragrant herb tastings or storytelling, might draw kin together, breathing warmth into this bustling heart of the city.
